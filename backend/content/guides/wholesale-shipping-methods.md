Freight is where wholesale margins are won or lost. A well-negotiated product price can be wiped out by the wrong shipping method. This guide compares every mainstream option for shipping wholesale orders from China, with the timelines and tradeoffs that matter in 2026.

## The four main shipping methods

### 1. International express (DHL / FedEx / UPS)

- **Best for:** samples, urgent replenishment, orders under 45 kg
- **Timeline:** 3–7 days door to door
- **Tradeoff:** highest per-kg cost; customs duties usually billed to the receiver

### 2. Air freight

- **Best for:** orders 45–300 kg where speed still matters
- **Timeline:** 8–12 days including customs clearance
- **Tradeoff:** about half the express price, but you handle or agent-handle clearance

### 3. Sea freight (LCL / FCL)

- **Best for:** bulk orders over 1–2 m³
- **Timeline:** 25–40 days to the US West Coast, 30–50 days to the EU
- **Tradeoff:** cheapest per kg by far, but slow and impractical for small batches

### 4. Rail freight (China → Europe)

- **Best for:** European buyers in the 300 kg–2 ton range
- **Timeline:** 18–25 days
- **Tradeoff:** a reliable middle path when sea is too slow and air too expensive

## Customs duties in practice

Duties depend on the destination country, product category, and declared value. Three rules that save money and delays:

1. Confirm the correct HS code **before** shipping — misclassification is the top cause of customs holds
2. Keep the commercial invoice consistent with your payment records
3. Ask your supplier for origin documentation; some product lines qualify for reduced tariffs

## Practical decision rule

If your order value is under $800 and you need it fast, use express. Between $800 and $5,000, air freight usually wins. Above that, or when volume exceeds 2 m³, ask for a sea freight quote and plan two months ahead.
